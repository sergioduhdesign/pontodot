Welcome to Catum.

The ideas beneath the world

Catum belongs to Monema, a fictional universe of three connected worlds: Amae, Yorrum, and Catum. Each exists within a sphere enclosed by an impenetrable energy boundary. From Catum, Amae and Yorrum appear as two moons: other worlds woven into the everyday sky.

Their relationship carries a question from the three-body problem: how does a system evolve when every body influences the others? In Monema, that question becomes a story about interdependence. Every world has its own identity. Every world changes the whole.

The names carry another layer.

The names

Monema

Meaning begins small.

The universe takes its name from a linguistic term associated with André Martinet: a minimal unit of meaning. A fitting beginning for a universe where small exchanges—a word, a question, a lesson—can become something much larger.

Amae

Adaptive Masked AutoEncoder.

This fictional expansion draws on masked autoencoders, which learn by reconstructing missing parts of an input. Amae carries the theme of perception: discovering relationships, making sense of fragments, learning to see beyond what is immediately visible.

Yorrum

Memory gives experience continuity.

The name combines an invented opening with RUM, Rotational Unit of Memory, a real research architecture for recurrent neural networks. In Monema, Yorrum represents what we carry forward: the experiences that connect yesterday’s discovery to tomorrow’s decision.

Catum

Cellular Automata for Turing Universal Modeling.

This fictional expansion draws on cellular automata: systems in which simple local rules can produce complex patterns. Some can even perform universal computation. Catum brings that idea into the lives of its inhabitants: small actions, repeated encounters, and shared learning gradually forming a community.

These scientific connections form the language of the fiction. The worlds and their expanded names belong to Monema’s mythology; the research beneath them gives that mythology its substance.

Perception. Memory. Action.

Three worlds, held in relation.

Together, they give Pontodot’s inhabitants a setting for the same journey they undertake with us: noticing, remembering, trying, and learning how to contribute.
